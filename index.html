Biography

Academic Biography

Salem A. Bahadi is a Ph.D. Candidate in Chemical Engineering at King Fahd University of Petroleum & Minerals (KFUPM), Saudi Arabia. He received his B.S. and M.Sc. degrees in Chemical Engineering from KFUPM and participated in a student exchange program at the University of Florida, United States.

His research focuses on advanced nanomaterials, adsorption, environmental remediation, drilling fluids, H₂S scavenging, complex fluid systems, and data-driven chemical engineering. His work includes the development of multicomponent adsorbents based on graphene, clay, layered hydroxides, and zeolitic imidazolate frameworks for wastewater treatment and energy-related applications.

His current Ph.D. research focuses on functionalized water- and oil-based drilling-fluid systems for H₂S removal, with particular attention to material functionality, rheological behavior, and operational performance.

His research also integrates experimental characterization with computational forecasting through hybrid artificial intelligence and ensemble machine-learning models. His experimental experience includes FT-IR, XRD, TGA, DLS/zeta potential, UV–Vis spectroscopy, and rheometry.

In addition to research, he has experience in laboratory instruction, teaching assistance, undergraduate research supervision, scientific peer review, and technical mentoring. He has supervised more than ten undergraduate researchers across projects involving wastewater treatment, drilling fluids, and H₂S scavenging.

Research Highlights

Water Remediation

Multifunctional materials for water decontamination

Developed multicomponent adsorbents including GO/Clay/MgFeAl-LTH and Layered Triple Hydroxide/ZIF nanocomposites for removing anionic and cationic dyes, phenolic compounds, and heavy metals from contaminated water.

Energy & Drilling

Functional drilling-fluid systems for H₂S control

Research on functionalized water- and oil-based drilling-fluid systems designed to enhance hydrogen sulfide scavenging while maintaining appropriate rheological and operational characteristics.

Data-Driven CHE

Machine learning for adsorption prediction

Applied optimal hybrid artificial-intelligence and ensemble meta-machine-learning approaches to predict adsorption behavior and support process-design optimization.

Complex Fluids

Biosurfactant-stabilized crude-oil nanoemulsions

Formulated and characterized crude oil-in-water nanoemulsions stabilized by environmentally friendly biosurfactants and investigated their stability and pH-switchable demulsification behavior.
