About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Chemical Engineering at King Fahd University of Petroleum & Minerals (KFUPM), with research experience spanning advanced nanomaterial synthesis, environmental remediation, adsorption processes, drilling fluids, H₂S scavenging, and computational modeling.

My work focuses on engineering multifunctional materials, including graphene-based composites, layered hydroxides, and zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs), and connecting their physicochemical characteristics with performance in environmental and energy-related applications.

I also integrate experimental research with hybrid artificial intelligence and ensemble machine-learning approaches to forecast adsorption behavior and support process optimization. My broader objective is to contribute to sustainable process engineering through materials innovation, rigorous experimentation, and predictive modeling.

Research Expertise

My research combines materials engineering, interfacial and adsorption phenomena, complex fluids, environmental applications, and computational prediction.

Advanced Materials & Adsorption

Design and characterization of graphene-based materials, layered hydroxides, ZIFs, and multicomponent nanocomposites for adsorption and separation applications.

02

Environmental Remediation

Development of adsorbents for the removal of dyes, phenolic compounds, heavy metals, and other organic and inorganic pollutants from contaminated water.

03

Drilling Fluids & H₂S Scavenging

Research Highlights

Water Remediation

Multifunctional adsorbents for contaminated water

Developed multicomponent materials including GO/Clay/MgFeAl-LTH and Layered Triple Hydroxide/ZIF nanocomposites for removing anionic and cationic dyes, phenolic compounds, and heavy metals from aqueous systems.

Energy & Drilling

Functional drilling fluids for H₂S control

Research on water- and oil-based drilling-fluid systems incorporating functional materials for hydrogen sulfide scavenging while maintaining appropriate rheological characteristics.

Data-Driven CHE

Machine learning for adsorption prediction

Applied optimal hybrid AI and ensemble meta-machine-learning models to predict adsorption of dyes and phenolic compounds, supporting reduced experimental dependency and improved process-design insight.
